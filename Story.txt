The cast-iron skillet hissed as a thick knob of salted butter hit the black surface, foaming instantly 
into a pale gold pool. Arthur cracked two eggs against the ceramic rim of the counter, dropping them 
softly into the pan. The white edges crisped to ruffled, golden lace almost immediately, bubbling 
eagerly around the bright, sun-like centers. Outside, the early morning light washed across the 
kitchen's wide oak floorboards, carrying the faint, cold scent of dew-heavy pine through the cracked 
window.

He lowered two slices of yesterday’s crusty sourdough into the toaster and turned back to his coffee 
pour-over. Dark roast dripped with steady, rhythmic patience into his favorite stoneware mug, filling 
the quiet air with the sharp, comforting aroma of roasted hazelnut and dark chocolate.

When the toast popped, hot and steam-kissed, he slathered one piece with dark plum jam and left the other 
plain for dipping. He slid the eggs onto a warmed plate, dusting them with flaky sea salt and freshly 
cracked black pepper. Sitting at the small pine table right where the sunlight pooled, he tore off a 
corner of toast, punctured the first yolk, and swept up the rich, warm velvet center.

It was a perfect symphony of simple textures: the shatter of toasted crust, the savory richness of 
yolk, and the deep, heat-driven kick of coffee resetting his palate between bites. Outside, the 
neighborhood was just beginning to stir—a car door shut down the street, a blue jay called from the 
fence post—but at the table, time paused. By the time he wiped the plate clean with the final bite, 
the day ahead felt completely, effortlessly manageable.
