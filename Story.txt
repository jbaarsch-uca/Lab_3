The cast-iron skillet hissed as a thick knob of salted butter hit the black surface, foaming instantly 
into a pale gold pool. Arthur cracked two eggs against the ceramic rim of the counter, dropping them 
softly into the pan. The white edges crisped to ruffled, golden lace almost immediately, bubbling 
eagerly around the bright, sun-like centers. Outside, the early morning light washed across the 
kitchen's wide oak floorboards, carrying the faint, cold scent of dew-heavy pine through the cracked 
window.

He lowered two slices of yesterday’s crusty sourdough into the toaster and turned back to his coffee 
pour-over. Dark roast dripped with steady, rhythmic patience into his favorite stoneware mug, filling 
the quiet air with the sharp, comforting aroma of roasted hazelnut and dark chocolate.

Suddenly something I hadn't expected to happen happened. And it would change his perspective on Geography forever.

A deep, guttural groan echoed from beneath the foundation, shaking the coffee right out of his cup. 
Before Arthur could react, the oak floorboards buckled violently upward and disintegrated. With a deafening 
roar and a blast of blinding heat, the floor beneath the table vanished into a churning chasm of incandescent 
molten lava.

Searing air slapped his face as a subterranean river of orange-red magma bubbled violently just feet beneath 
his dangling slippers. The kitchen walls crumbled outward, but by some absurd miracle of physics, Arthur’s 
chair remained balanced on a single, stubborn wooden beam bridging the fiery abyss.

Sulphurous steam hissed against the cool morning breeze drifting through the blown-out window. Arthur sat 
frozen, his eyes watering from the instant heat spike. He looked down at the roaring liquid fire swirling 
beneath his toes, then slowly lowered his gaze back to his plate.

The intense radiant heat below was actually keeping his eggs perfectly warm.  He was looking forward to eating 
them as soon as he got a moment.

Arthur took a steadying breath, carefully shifted his weight, and took a sip of his coffee. He swept his toast 
through the yolk and took a bite. If the Earth's mantle was going to swallow his mortgage, it would just have 
to wait until he finished eating.
